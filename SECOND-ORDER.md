| 17 | F | A second, harder tier of missions was added at Sam's request: each teaching chapter keeps its short one-/two-choice intro levels and gains three advanced follow-ups that ramp to one technical finale, where the student configures a larger network (more machines, more hops, more loops) — "aping" the device's own decisions through click-choice edits, never typed addresses. Link: `crowd` (collision domains over four senders and two boxes), `backbone` (two redundant loops to block), `floor` (kinds + a loop at once). Network: `point` (gateway + one route), `roads` (two prefixes down two roads), `across` (a four-router chain plus a TTL big enough for the hops). Transport: `window` (window/timeout on a lossier 8-part file), `connect` (the handshake across a router — TCP is end-to-end, so routing it changes nothing), `reliable` (a routed, lossy go-back-N transfer tuned to a deadline). Nineteen levels now, 57 stars; `tests/browser/interactions.py` counts updated (19 listed / 18 locked / 17 after the first pass). Three gotchas, each watched fail then fixed in `campaign.rs`: (a) `crowd` — a server never transmits, so the switches never learn its port and *flood every frame*; four senders at once saturated the back box's out-queue (`PORT_QUEUE`), dropping sales — seen as "Register C's sales all arrive: not met, 2 frames lost at Back box's outgoing line"; fixed by staggering the sends so the flood never exceeds the queue. (b) `floor` — that same flooding makes a clean, loss-free run cross 63 frames, so the first `max_frames` ceiling of 30 failed the honest solution ("63 frames crossed … not met"); raised to 90, well under the hundreds an open loop makes. (c) `across` — three routers each decrement TTL, so a packet needs ≥4 hops of life; the options start at 2 (fails in transit) and the quiz turns on exactly that. Window/timeout deadlines for `window` (≤70 ticks) and `reliable` (≤110) were set from a throwaway matrix over every window×timeout combo, then the matrix was deleted. |
